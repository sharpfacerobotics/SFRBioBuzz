## Goals

BIOBUZZ rewards three things software can directly help with:

1. **Accurate launching into a moving target.** Only the upward-facing CELL scores, and each
   HIVE TIP (20 pts) flips which cell that is.
2. **Fast, legal cycles.** No more than 4 elements at a time (G407), and never the opponent's
   NECTAR (G408).
3. **Reliable autonomous points.** LEAVE, PARK in the LOADING ZONE, and launching the 4 pre-loads,
   all without crossing to the opponent's half (G402).

We set three software goals: **the driver should never have to aim, the robot should never
break a rule by itself, and every match should leave data we can learn from.**

## Architecture

One class per mechanism, and one OpMode that connects them. We wrote a team protocol so five
programmers could work in parallel without merge conflicts or hidden coupling:

- **Every subsystem takes `RobotHardware` in its constructor** and only touches its own hardware.
- **Subsystems never call each other.** Data is passed in as parameters by the OpMode, so each
  class can be read, tested and owned by one person. We check this automatically by inspecting
  the compiled bytecode.
- **No allocation in the control loop, and no `Math.pow` for squares or cubes**, so loop times
  stay consistent.

```
 Pedro follower ─ pose, velocity ─┐
 HiveVision ─ tags, area ─────────┼─► ShooterConfig ─ turret angle ─► Turret ─ on target ─┐

 Storage ─ next element ─► Launcher                    Launcher ─ shot detected ─► Storage
```

## Key design decisions

**Shoot-on-the-move.** A launched element keeps the robot's velocity. ShooterConfig aims at a
*virtual goal*, `goal − robotVelocity × timeOfFlight`. Time of flight depends on distance, and
distance depends on the virtual goal, so we iterate three times, which converges well within the
size of the cell. Flywheel speed and hood angle come from a measured distance table, using
shape-preserving (PCHIP) interpolation so the curve never overshoots between data points.

**Tracking the moving target.** We modeled the hive's tipping from the official field
calibration spec: a hive tips at 8 POLLEN, or 3 POLLEN + 3 NECTAR, so one NECTAR weighs about 5/3
of a POLLEN. The upward cell starts with 3 NECTAR, so our model predicts **the 3rd pre-load tips
it**. Auto then pauses for the hive to settle and sends the 4th pre-load to the other cell.
Each cell's AprilTag cluster is on its underside, facing down.
The cell facing up therefore shows its tags to our camera, and a tipped cell hides them. When
we keep seeing the *other* cell's tags and not the target's, `HiveTracker` decides the hive has
tipped and switches targets, even when our partner caused the tip. Driver 2 can override it
with one button.

**A turret that can't hurt itself.** On startup the turret finds its magnetic zero with a
"cow-path" search: it sweeps out and back, doubling the sweep each time, capped at the hard
stops and with a timeout. When aiming, it picks whichever equivalent angle (θ, θ ± 360°) stays
inside its soft limits, and unwinds the long way rather than wrapping its cable. Feedforward
on the chassis turn rate keeps it on target while the robot spins.

**Rules enforced in code.** Storage keeps an ordered record of what's inside from a color
sensor. It refuses to count the opponent's NECTAR or a 5th element, and the intake spits them
straight back out (G407, G408). Every auto path is checked to stay on our half (G402), and a
unit test fails if one doesn't.

**Detecting shots without a sensor.** An element leaving the launcher causes a sharp dip in
flywheel speed. `ShotDetector` counts a shot only when the speed drops 120 ticks/s within 0.15 s
of being at full speed. This rejects false shots caused by changing the target speed while
driving, which our first version miscounted.

**One auto, two alliances.** The field is 180° rotationally symmetric, not mirrored. Each auto
is written once for red, and blue is generated by rotating every pose. This removes a whole
class of copy-paste errors.

## Driver experience

- Auto-aim with auto spin-up, plus an optional **auto-fire** that shoots whenever the robot is
  loaded, in range and ready.
- **Fallbacks** if the turret fails: manual turret control on a stick, or turning the whole
  chassis toward the hive.
- **Park assist:** hold B and Pedro Pathing drives the robot into the LOADING ZONE for 5 pts.
- **Feedback on the controllers:** rumble when storage is full, at 60 s (FLOWERS open), and at
  15 s (time to park). The controller light shows when a shot is ready.

## Testing and verification

- **47 unit tests** run on a laptop with no robot: interpolation, motion profiles, shot
  detection, shooter geometry, turret wrap-around, color classification, hive tip detection,
  field symmetry, and **every auto checked against G304/G402 and the PARK/LEAVE rules**.
- **Pit OpModes:** `SubsystemTest` exercises each mechanism and sensor, `TurretTuner` shows a
  live step response, and `ShotTableTuner` builds the distance table on the field.
- **Code review.** A structured review of our first version found 10 bugs before they reached
  the robot, including gate timing that would have jammed elements and a pose hand-off that
  could be lost between auto and TeleOp.

## Learning from every match

Every OpMode writes a 20 Hz CSV log to the Control Hub. `tools/analyze_matchlog.py` turns it
into cycle times, shots per cycle, flywheel recovery time, how often the flywheel or turret was
the bottleneck, battery sag and loop time.
